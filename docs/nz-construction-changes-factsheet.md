# NZ construction sector changes — verified fact sheet

**Compiled 9 August 2026.** Every fact below was read from a primary source on
that date. This file is the ONLY permitted factual basis for the `/insights/`
collection. Do not add facts from memory, from search summaries, or from
competitor blogs. If a drafter needs a fact that is not here, the answer is to
leave it out, not to reconstruct it.

## Why this file exists

Three separate fabrications made it into drafts earlier in this build: a
trademark conflict that did not verify, a client outcome not present in the
source, and delivery-mechanism claims made in two opposite directions. All
three were plausible and all three were wrong. A fact sheet compiled once and
checked once is cheaper than catching the same class of error four more times.

## The single most important framing point

**Almost none of this is law yet.** Both relevant bills passed only their
FIRST READING, on 19 November 2025. A first reading is the beginning of the
parliamentary process, not the end. Content that implies a tradie must act now,
or that these rules currently apply, is wrong and would damage the brand more
than the traffic is worth.

Correct: "is proposed", "would, if passed", "the Government has said it intends".
Wrong: "new rules require", "from 2026 you must", "the law now says".

## 1. Liability and consumer protection (MBIE)

Source: https://www.mbie.govt.nz/about/news/new-liability-rules-for-the-construction-sector-to-improve-consumer-protection

- Liability model moves from **joint and several** to **proportionate** — each
  party "only required to pay their fair share for any defective work they
  contributed to."
- **Mandatory home warranties** for new residential buildings **three storeys
  and under**, and renovations **$100,000 and above**. Cover is a **1-year
  defect period plus a 10-year structural warranty**.
- **Professional indemnity insurance** required for **design professionals
  (architects, engineers)**. Explicitly **does NOT apply to builders.** Do not
  blur this — it is the detail a reader would catch.
- **LBP disciplinary penalties**: maximum fine **$10,000 → $20,000**; maximum
  suspension **12 months → 24 months**.
- Timing: Building Amendment Bill expected to be introduced **early 2026**;
  roughly a **1-year implementation period** after it passes; proportionate
  liability and the new requirements operational **around 2027**.

## 2. Small standalone dwellings — consent exemption

Source: https://www.lbp.govt.nz/about-us/news-and-updates/update-on-building-consent-for-small-standalone-dwellings/

- **Not law. Proposed.** Targeted to take effect **2026**.
- Dwellings **up to 70 square metres**.
- Must have **a simple design** and **meet the Building Code**.
- Work must be **carried out or supervised by the relevant licensed
  tradesperson**.
- Homeowners must **notify their local council before construction begins and
  again after completion**.
- Until it takes effect, **all small standalone dwellings still require
  building consent.**

## 3. Plumber and drainlayer self-certification

Source: https://www.mbie.govt.nz/about/news/legislation-moves-forward-on-plumber-and-drainlayer-self-certification-and-stronger-licensed-building-practitioners-licensing

- Building and Construction Sector (Self-certification by Plumbers and
  Drainlayers) Amendment Bill — **passed first reading 19 November 2025**.
  **No implementation date announced.**
- Would let **approved** plumbers and drainlayers **self-certify
  straightforward work** without building consent authority inspections.
- Only those **meeting specific eligibility criteria** could participate; the
  **Plumbers, Gasfitters and Drainlayers (PGD) Board** sets and enforces the
  competency and compliance standards.
- Stated intent: reduce delays, let authorities focus on complex projects.

## 4. Strengthening occupational licensing

Source: https://www.lbp.govt.nz/about-us/news-and-updates/building-and-construction-sector-strengthening-occupational-licensing-regimes-amendment-bill/

- Building and Construction (Strengthening Occupational Licensing Regimes)
  Amendment Bill — **passed first reading 19 November 2025. No implementation
  date announced.**
- Covers **LBPs, licensed electrical workers, and plumbers/gasfitters/
  drainlayers**.
- Contents: expanded **Registrar powers** for complaint management; increased
  **transparency about suspensions and cancellations**; **streamlined complaint
  handling**; new professional **Codes of Ethics**.

## What we are NOT allowed to say

**No demand-side statistics.** Research surfaced claims that homeowners in 2026
expect faster replies, are more comparison-driven, and prefer digital-first
contact. Every one of those traced back to an Australian marketing blog
(serviceseeking.com.au) or a New Zealand lead-generation agency's own content —
i.e. competitors marketing their own services. They are not evidence. This is
the same call already made six times on this project against the circulating
"62% of calls go unanswered" and "$100–200 per missed call" figures. Do not use
them, do not paraphrase them, do not use them "directionally".

**No compliance advice.** This is the hard boundary. The site's own trust band
states plainly that the business holds **IT and systems credentials, not trade
qualifications**. A post explaining what a builder must do to comply would
contradict that within a week of publishing it.

## The angle that IS in our lane

Write about the **operational consequence for enquiry handling**, never the
compliance obligation.

- Proportionate liability + a 10-year structural warranty makes **proving what
  was agreed, and when** commercially decisive. A timestamped written record of
  every enquiry, quote and promise is exactly what a front desk produces. This
  is honest, useful, and squarely our subject.
- The 70m² exemption, if it happens, is an **enquiry-volume** story for
  builders — more small-dwelling enquiries arriving — not a building-code story.
- Self-certification, if it happens, changes the **job mix and scheduling
  rhythm** for plumbers and drainlayers, which changes what their front desk
  has to triage.

Every post must include, in its own words, a line to the effect of: we are not
qualified to advise on compliance, talk to your licensing body or your lawyer.
Say it plainly, once, without hedging around it.
