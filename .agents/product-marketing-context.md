## Product Overview

**One-liner:** The front desk your trade business should already have — installed and run for you.

**What it does:** Tradie Front Desk is a done-for-you, managed front-desk system for trade businesses. It captures every enquiry (missed calls, website forms, texts, social and Google messages, old leads), replies fast while the tradie is still on the tools, qualifies the job, moves good-fit customers toward a callback, quote, or site visit, and follows up on quotes and quiet leads. The owner keeps control of pricing, promises, and which jobs they take.

**Core mechanism:** Capture → Respond → Qualify → Book → Follow Up

**Product category:** Managed front desk system for tradies. This is a category the brand is *creating* — buyers do not search for it by name. They search around the symptoms (missed calls, slow replies, quote follow-up) and around the adjacent categories they already know (answering service, receptionist, lead generation, trade CRM). SEO must meet them in those known categories.

**Product type:** Done-for-you managed service (setup fee + monthly retainer). Not software, not a SaaS login, not a lead marketplace.

**Business model:** Separate setup fee and monthly fee, tiered by complexity:

| Package | Price | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Front Desk Starter | From $1,500 setup + $497/mo | Smaller businesses needing missed-call recovery, fast replies, simple follow-up |
| Booked Jobs System *(flagship)* | From $2,500 setup + $1,200/mo | Established tradies needing capture, qualification, booking, follow-up, tracking |
| Managed Growth System | From $4,000 setup + $2,000/mo + ad spend | Growth-focused businesses adding volume once the front desk works |

Add-ons: old-lead reactivation, phone answering, local campaign booster, website enquiry upgrade.

**Parent brand:** System Pros AI (byline "by System Pros AI"). Legal entities: System Pros AI, The AI and Automation Agency LTD, Altcutm LLC.

## Target Audience

**Target companies:** Trade and home-service businesses — 3–20 staff, owner-operated or founder-led, high-value services, local or regional service area, already receiving phone and website enquiries, with capacity to take more work. Secondary fit: solo operators with high-value jobs and growth ambition; larger trade businesses with poor enquiry handling.

**Priority trades** (site currently has pages for the first three): electricians, plumbers/drainage, roofers. Wider target list: HVAC, solar installers, landscapers, fencing, renovation, builders, painters, concrete, earthmoving, pest control, garage doors, insulation, flooring, bathroom/kitchen renovation, glaziers, security installers.

**Decision-makers:** Owner / founder / managing director is the primary buyer. Secondary influencer: the office support person (partner, admin, dispatcher) who actually feels the chaos day to day. The site speaks to the owner.

**Technical ability:** Low to moderate. Comfortable with phone, email, Facebook, Google Business Profile, text, and possibly Xero / Tradify / ServiceM8 / Fergus / Simpro. Not comfortable with CRM automation, ad platforms, funnels, integrations, analytics, dashboards. **This directly constrains keyword targeting — they do not search in software vocabulary.**

**Primary use case:** Stop losing already-paid-for enquiries to missed calls, slow replies, and dead quotes.

**Jobs to be done:**
- "Answer the customer when I physically cannot pick up the phone."
- "Chase my quotes so I stop losing jobs I already priced."
- "Make my business look organised and professional without me hiring admin."

**Service area: New Zealand and Australia.** Confirmed by the client 7 Aug 2026. NZ is the established market — all current site copy, the primary phone number, and the only delivery history. AU is expansion: an AU phone number exists, but there is no AU content, no AU client, no AU address, and no AU proof. Treat NZ as the authority base and AU as a market being entered from zero. The US number in the footer is not a target market for this work.

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:** Trade businesses do not have a demand problem first — they have an enquiry-handling problem. Missed calls, slow replies, and dead quotes lose jobs they already paid to generate.

**Why alternatives fall short:**
- **Do nothing** — enquiries keep leaking; everything depends on the owner's memory
- **Buy leads** — shared, inconsistent, customer is shopping around, no asset is built, dependency grows
- **Ad agency** — optimises for leads not booked jobs; calls still missed, follow-up still poor; often creates more admin
- **Hire admin/receptionist** — expensive, slow to hire and train, no after-hours cover, people forget follow-ups too, systems still need building
- **DIY software** — too much setup and jargon, owner has no time, automations built wrong, gets abandoned

**What it costs them:** Missed calls, delayed replies, lost quotes, no customer database, wasted ad spend, repeat customers never reactivated.

**Emotional tension:** Overwhelmed, behind, guilty for not replying, embarrassed by slow replies, suspicious of agencies, burned before, nervous about wasting money again. Status pain: "my business looks less professional than my competitors" and "I'm still running everything off my phone."

## Competitive Landscape

**Direct:** Other done-for-you front-desk / missed-call-text-back operators and trade-focused marketing agencies offering managed lead handling — falls short by leading with software or ads rather than the managed process.
**Secondary:** Answering services and virtual receptionists — falls short on after-hours cover, quote follow-up, and pipeline tracking; handles the call but not the job.
**Indirect:** Shared-lead sellers (falls short: rented, shared, no asset) and trade job software like ServiceM8/Tradify/Fergus/Simpro (falls short: it manages jobs you already won, not enquiries you haven't answered — and is another login to run yourself).

⚠ Named direct competitors have not been researched. Phase 1 SERP teardown will populate this properly.

## Differentiation

**Key differentiators:**
- Managed and run for the client — not software they have to operate
- Fixes enquiry *handling* before adding enquiry *volume* (ads are optional and come later)
- Owner keeps control of pricing, promises, and job selection
- Sits alongside existing job software rather than replacing it

**Why customers choose us:** It's the layer before hiring admin and before spending more on ads — the cheapest fix is answering the enquiries you already get.

**Positioning lines that work:**
> The jobs you lose are often not the ones you never got. They are the ones you received but did not respond to, follow up, or organise properly.

> Before you spend more money getting enquiries, make sure your business can handle and convert the enquiries it already receives.

> Stop renting leads. Own the system that captures and converts your own job opportunities.

> You do not need another login. You need the system installed, tested, and managed for you.

## Objections

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "I already get enough enquiries." | This isn't only about more leads — it's about the enquiries you already get being captured, followed up, and organised. Busy businesses need less chaos, not more of it. |
| "I don't want to run ads." | Ads are optional and usually only make sense once the front desk can handle new enquiries fast. |
| "I don't want a machine talking to my customers." | It runs on approved texts, emails, forms, and follow-up. Any phone answering is optional, controlled, and uses scripts you sign off. |
| "It's too expensive." | Don't compare it to software — compare it to missed calls, lost quotes, and the cost of hiring admin. Anchor against hiring, not against SaaS. Never discount on the spot. |
| "Can you guarantee more jobs?" | No. We guarantee the agreed system gets built, tested, launched, and fixed if a listed part isn't working. Never guarantee job numbers, revenue, or customer behaviour. |

**Anti-persona:** Brand-new businesses with no budget; fully-booked solo operators who don't want growth; very low average job value; owners who refuse to use any system; no capacity to take more work; poor service quality or reputation; expecting guaranteed jobs without participating; only want free leads.

## Switching Dynamics

**Push:** A specific job lost — the quote that went quiet, the missed call that went to the next tradie. Burned by a previous agency or lead seller.
**Pull:** "Someone gets it and will just handle this." Relief, not excitement.
**Habit:** Everything runs off the owner's phone and memory; it has always worked well enough.
**Anxiety:** Being sold software they'll have to run; a machine saying the wrong thing to their customer; wasting money again; losing control of pricing and promises.

## Customer Language

**How they describe the problem:**
- "I need more good jobs."
- "I do not want more admin."
- "I am sick of chasing people."
- "I do not want to waste money on ads."
- "I have been burned before."

**Words to use:** front desk, enquiries, quotes, missed calls, follow-up, booked jobs, on the tools, job, callback, site visit, chasing, organised.

**Words to avoid — still banned outright in all buyer-facing copy:** CRM, automation, funnel, SaaS, workflow, GoHighLevel, bot. Sell the system and the outcome, never the tooling underneath.

**"AI" — restricted, not banned (client decision, 8 Aug 2026).** Allowed in meta titles/descriptions, FAQ questions that mirror buyer phrasing, and a dedicated comparison page targeting the "AI receptionist" query. **Never** in hero headlines, CTAs, or section headings on core pages — those stay problem-led (missed calls, dead quotes, slow replies). The term is a doorway for search visibility, never the pitch. Full boundary table in `site-v3/PRODUCT.md` → Brand Personality. Rationale: every close competitor leads with the term and the query family is the fastest-growing in the category, but ranking for it attracts software shoppers who convert badly against a done-for-you service — hence restricted rather than open.

⚠ These verbatims are from the strategy docs, not from recorded sales calls. Real call transcripts would materially improve copy — see Open Questions #3.

## Brand Voice

**Tone:** Confident, plain-spoken, specific.
**Style:** Short sentences, plain English, direct explanations, confident recommendations, clear next steps. Diagnostic and calm under sales pressure — *"I would not start with ads yet. Your first issue is that enquiries are not being followed up properly."*
**Personality:** Operator first, advisor second. Never a hype marketer, never flashy, gimmicky, corporate, or robotic.
**Emotional goal:** The reader recognises their own business in the problem in the first fold, then feels relief that someone will just handle it.
